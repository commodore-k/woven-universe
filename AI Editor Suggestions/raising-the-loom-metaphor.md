# What "raising" comprehensibly *does* (the loom / slack)

- **Status:** proposed
- **Target:** `Weave Theory AI.md` §4 (Power, Heft & Raising) — optional intuition/flavor

## Suggestion
Give "raising" a grounded *function* without explaining its *nature*:

> You cannot re-tie a knot in a rope that is under full load — you need slack. A Weave at rest is **taut on its loom**; its strands cannot be worked. **Raising** slackens and exposes the strands enough that an agent can grip and manipulate the actual Warp & Weft. When the raise ends, the Weave settles back onto its loom and sets.

## Why it works
It explains *why* raising is the prerequisite to every constructive tie (you can't work loaded fabric) and *why* it must be held for the whole operation (let go and it snaps taut mid-tie → the Weave "drops"). It is honest textile physics, so it feels inevitable rather than invented — yet it says nothing about what the lifted state *is* dimensionally, so the mystery survives intact.

## How to apply
Either a one-line aside in §4, or leave it here as flavor the narration can lean on when a character raises a Weave. Keep the "dimensional nature is out of scope" line either way.
